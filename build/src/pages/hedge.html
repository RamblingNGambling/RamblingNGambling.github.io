How to read it

Leave the hedge stake empty and the tool fills in the amount that makes both outcomes pay the same. That amount is the equal hedge, and the locked figure is your profit whichever way the game goes.

Type your own hedge stake to take less than the full lock. The two outcome figures then differ, and the locked figure shows the worse one.

Add the book’s cash-out offer to see which is better. The offer is the money returned to you, stake included.

When to hedge

It is the right move when the remaining edge is small next to the money at stake. It is the wrong move when you still hold the edge: if the current price on the other side is worse than fair, a full hedge sells your position at a loss to the market.

Cash out is a hedge with a fee

A book’s cash-out is the same trade, priced by the book. It is almost always worse than hedging by hand, because the book takes its margin on both the original price and the current one. Put the offer in the field and the tool tells you the gap in money.

Partial hedges

You do not have to lock the whole thing. Put in half the equal hedge and you keep half the upside and remove half the risk. The two outcome figures show exactly where that leaves you.
