The record / How to read it

What the numbers mean

A losing win rate can still be a winning record. This page explains why, and what each figure on the tracker is actually counting.

Start with the unit

A unit is one standard bet. Not a dollar amount, a size. Every figure on this site is in units, so the record reads the same whether somebody bets $5 or $500 a unit. A 0.5u play is half a normal bet. A 2u play is double.

This is the honest way to publish a record. A tout who posts dollars is telling you about the size of their bankroll, not the quality of their bets.

Why a losing win rate can win money

The record on this page is under 50% wins. That is normal and it is not a problem, because not every bet pays the same.

At −110, the standard price, you risk 110 to win 100. You need to win about 52.4% of those just to break even. At +150 you risk 100 to win 150, and you only need 40% to break even. A book of plays at longer prices wins less often and still makes money.

So the win rate on its own tells you almost nothing. What matters is whether the price was better than the true chance. That is what ROI measures.

ROI is the number that counts

ROI here is profit divided by everything risked. Not profit over a bankroll, and not profit over the number of bets: over the units actually put at risk. Voided stakes are excluded. Push stakes are included.

A positive ROI over a long run means the prices taken were better than the outcomes deserved. That is the only claim this page makes.

Read the sample, not just the number

A few hundred plays is a small sample. Anyone can run 13% ROI over two months and be flat over two years. The page says how many settled plays it is standing on, and that figure matters as much as the ROI beside it.

Treat a short record as a record, not as a rate. Come back when the count is larger.

Pending, push and void

Pending is posted but not settled. Those plays are not in the record until they land.

Push is a tie against the line. The stake comes back and it counts as neither a win nor a loss, which is why the record has three numbers.

Void is a bet the book cancelled or refunded. It stays in the log. It is shown separately from pushes and excluded from the record, win rate and ROI stake total.

How to check any of it

Most plays in the log link to the post that called them, marked with an X at the end of the row. Click one and it opens the original post, with its own timestamp, from before the event started.

The site itself is a file in a public Git repository, so every update carries a commit time that cannot be moved afterwards. The whole log is also published as a plain data file, plays.json, if you would rather add it up yourself.

What this is not

It is not advice, it is not a service, and nothing here is for sale. It is a record of plays that were posted in public, kept honestly, including the parts that lost.
